IQ/OQ Validation Documentation for Slitter Rewinders: What Regulated Buyers Should Demand, and When

Medical materials, pharmaceutical packaging and battery separators are converted under quality systems — the machine must arrive with documented evidence, not just performance claims.

Published September 25, 2026 | By HDPTH Technical Editorial Team

Short answer: if your slitter rewinder feeds medical, pharmaceutical packaging or battery separator production, insist on executed IQ and OQ documentation as a contractual deliverable. Installation Qualification proves the machine matches the specification as installed; Operational Qualification proves its functions work within stated limits across the intended range. Tie OQ protocols to the FAT, verify installation with IQ at SAT, and agree the deliverables list — protocols, raw data, calibration certificates, software records, punch-list closure — before the order is signed.

Buyers in regulated sectors do not purchase a slitter rewinder only for its cutting quality — they purchase evidence. Their quality systems, audited under ISO 13485 for medical devices, GMP expectations for pharmaceutical packaging, or customer-specific schemes for battery separators, require documented proof that equipment is specified, installed and operating as intended. That proof is produced through qualification: IQ, OQ and, at the buyer’s site, PQ. A supplier who cannot deliver it becomes the audit finding.

What IQ and OQ actually cover

The two qualifications answer different questions:

  • IQ — is the machine what was ordered and correctly installed? Verified drawings and component lists, utility connections, product-contact material certificates, safety devices present and set, calibration status of onboard instruments.
  • OQ — does every function work within its specified limits? Recorded tests across the intended range: minimum and maximum web tension, line speed, slitting width accuracy, rewind torque profiles, splicing, alarms and interlocks, E-stops.

Performance Qualification on your actual product remains your site’s task, but it builds directly on the OQ evidence — which is why the quality of that evidence matters commercially.

Linking FAT and SAT to the qualification plans

The efficient structure runs qualification through the acceptance visits. At the factory acceptance test, the supplier executes the OQ protocols on the completed machine — same test plans and criteria that will appear in the documentation package, witnessed and signed. At the site installation and commissioning stage, IQ is verified against the as-delivered machine, and critical OQ tests are repeated with site utilities, your materials and your environment. This division of labour avoids duplicating every test and catches transport or installation deviations where they happen. The split must be agreed when the contract is written; retrofitting protocols after delivery is slow and contentious.

Writing the buyer checklist

Four commitments convert validation from a promise into a plan. First, protocols drafted at order placement, so the FAT tests the real document rather than improvising one. Second, raw data included — recorded tension curves, width measurements at every lane, alarm response times — not just pass stamps. Third, a defined review period for your quality team with supplier response obligations, and named document owners on both sides. Fourth, deviations handled transparently: classified, dispositioned and closed before final payment milestones. Frequently asked questions

What do IQ and OQ mean for a slitter rewinder?

Installation Qualification documents that the machine as delivered and installed matches the agreed specification — utilities, dimensions, safety devices and component list verified against drawings. Operational Qualification demonstrates, with recorded tests, that every function operates within its specified limits across the whole intended range: tension floors and ceilings, speed, slitting width, rewind torque and alarms. Together they establish documented evidence that the machine is built and behaves as specified; PQ on your product follows at your site.

Which sectors require validation documentation for slitting equipment?

Medical device materials, pharmaceutical packaging, and battery separator production are the main ones — any sector where the conversion process is part of a quality management system, under ISO 13485, GMP expectations or equivalent customer audits. Even outside regulated sectors, buyers increasingly ask for structured documentation because it shortens their own audits and protects traceability when claims arise.

How do FAT and SAT link to IQ/OQ testing?

Well-planned, the FAT executes the OQ protocols on the supplier’s floor — same test plans, same acceptance criteria, same instruments — so SAT at your site then verifies installation (IQ) and repeats critical OQ tests with site utilities and your materials. This division avoids running everything twice: the FAT is the functional evidence, the SAT confirms nothing changed in transport, installation and site conditions. Agree the protocol split before ordering, not after arrival.

What documentation deliverables should the supplier commit to?

A technical file with general arrangement and utility drawings, a bill of materials for critical parts, materials and surface-finish certificates for product-contact parts, calibration certificates for instruments used in testing, safety and risk assessment documentation, software version records, executed IQ and OQ protocols with raw test data, and a punch list showing deviations closed. Define the language, format, review period and the consequence of late or incomplete delivery in the contract.

How long does the validation documentation process take, and who is responsible?

Documentation review runs alongside manufacturing and typically adds weeks rather than days, depending on how many review rounds the buyer needs. Responsibility is shared: the supplier drafts protocols and executes FAT tests, the buyer reviews, witnesses and approves, and both should nominate a named owner. Starting protocol drafting at order placement — not at machine completion — is the single best predictor of an on-time, documented handover.
