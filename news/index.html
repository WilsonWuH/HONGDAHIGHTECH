1Tissue Asset ConsolidationJuly 2, 2026

Arbex Takes Control of 22 Tissue and Hygiene Plants

Suzano completed its purchase of 51% of Kimberly-Clark's international Family Care and Professional business, creating Arbex. The independent company now owns 22 manufacturing sites in 14 countries and supplies consumer and professional tissue products across more than 70 markets.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: A network this large will compare output and quality across plants, increasing pressure for standardized roll specifications, automation, traceability and retrofit-ready converting equipment.

Source: PaperAge on the launch of Arbex

2Napkin Converting InvestmentJuly 2, 2026

Duni Poppies Adds Three Napkin Converting Lines

Duni Poppies said it installed three new Omet production lines during the past 12 months and expanded warehouse space by more than 30%. Its St Helens operation now covers 12 acres, runs more than 40 production lines and supplies tissue napkins and premium airlaid tabletop products.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: The investment shows that profitable growth depends on linking printing and converting capacity with material handling and warehouse flow, not simply adding line speed.

Source: Tissue World on Duni Poppies' converting expansion

3Tissue Machine RebuildJune 29, 2026

ICT Poland Rebuild Raises Output and Cuts Energy

ICT Poland restarted TM11 after an ANDRITZ rebuild covering the shoe press and reel automation. The company reports 2.5% higher gross daily production, 5% lower specific steam, gas and electricity use, plus improved softness and caliper.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Better parent reels only create value if downstream equipment preserves bulk and softness, so rewind pressure, tension taper and roll-density recipes should be validated after mill upgrades.

Source: ANDRITZ on the ICT Poland TM11 rebuild

4UK Tissue CapacityJuly 2, 2026

Five UK Tissue Machines Are Scheduled to Start in 2026

A current Tissue World and ResourceWise market assessment says five tissue machines are scheduled to start in the United Kingdom during 2026, with more starts expected in 2027 and 2028. The additions follow a long period of mill closures and import dependence.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: More domestic parent-reel supply can shorten lead times and shift grade economics, so UK converters should reassess reel widths, inventory policy and whether existing slit, rewind and packaging capacity can absorb the new supply.

Source: Tissue World on planned UK tissue-machine starts

5Carton Input CostsMay 12, 2026

Nippon Paper Raises Liquid-Carton Prices by More Than 10%

Nippon Paper Industries implemented a price increase of more than 10% for liquid-packaging carton and auxiliary materials on deliveries from July 1. The company cited sharp increases in essential raw-material costs that could no longer be absorbed internally.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Double-digit substrate increases make waste, trim loss and rejected rolls materially more expensive, strengthening the case for precise slitting, web inspection and repeatable tension control.

Source: Nippon Paper Industries price-revision notice

1Wet-Wipe Process ControlJuly 1, 2026

Wipes Study Links Formula Complexity to Contamination Risk

Clariant presented research at World of Wipes on how the number and chemical nature of wet-wipe ingredients affect preservative robustness and susceptibility to microbial failure. Moist, nutrient-rich wipe systems require preservation that remains effective through manufacturing, storage and use.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Substrate conversion cannot be separated from liquid handling; buyers should specify hygienic design, accurate dosing, controlled wet pick-up, batch records and cleanable changeover procedures.

Source: Clariant's World of Wipes presentation

2Wipes Product InnovationJune 4, 2026

Three Wipes Products Compete on Function and Substrate Design

INDA's 2026 World of Wipes Innovation Award finalists are Clorox Refreshables, Lenzing DualWipe and Rockline First Defense Fentanyl Detection Wipe. The shortlist spans reusable-function concepts, a two-sided bio-based cleaning substrate and a specialized detection application.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Differentiated wipes require flexible lines that can control substrate orientation, dosing, folding, perforation and pack format while preventing cross-contamination between products.

Source: Kohan Textile Journal on the 2026 WOW finalists

3Hydroentangling TechnologyApril 13, 2026

Compact AquaJet Targets Lower-Cost Wipes Production

Trutzschler introduced a compact, energy-efficient AquaJet hydroentangling machine for dry and wet wipe nonwovens. The launch accompanies new X-Series cards and digital tools for energy tracking and camera-based anomaly detection.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Compact upstream equipment can lower entry barriers, but stable finished rolls still depend on uniform basis weight, early defect detection and coordinated slitting and winding downstream.

Source: Trutzschler on its compact AquaJet and digital controls

4Filtration NonwovensApril 2, 2026

FILTECH Focuses on High-Efficiency Meltblown and Spunbond

Barmag presented meltblown and spunbond production options for filter media at FILTECH, held June 30 to July 2. Its hydrocharging unit is designed to reduce pressure drop while maintaining high filtration efficiency, and the meltblown platform can process polymers ranging from PP and PE to PET, PLA and specialty materials.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Filter media performance depends on uniform web structure and clean downstream handling, so slitting dust, static, edge damage and roll compression must be controlled as tightly as basis weight.

Source: Barmag on its FILTECH 2026 nonwoven technologies

5PET Spunbond SourcingMay 15, 2026

EU Duties Reset PET Spunbond Sourcing Costs

The European Commission imposed provisional anti-dumping duties of 45.6% to 50.0% on PET spunbond imports from China after an investigation into injury to EU producers. EDANA welcomed the measure as support for regional production and investment.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: A large landed-cost change can alter supplier choices and web properties quickly, so converters need equipment recipes and incoming-roll tests that support controlled qualification of alternative PET spunbond sources.

Source: EDANA on provisional EU PET spunbond duties

1Pouch Packaging DemandJune 24, 2026

Mars Opens 290-Million-Pouch Pet Food Facility

Mars opened an AU$112.6 million wet cat-food pouch facility in Wodonga, Australia, with annual capacity of about 290 million pouches. The site includes automated packaging, autonomous mobile robots and selected AI-enabled planning tools.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: High-volume pouch filling creates sustained demand for flat, defect-free film rolls; suppliers should control splice quality, winding tension, edge condition and roll genealogy at production speed.

Source: Packaging Insights on the Mars Wodonga pouch facility

2Express Packaging RulesJuly 2, 2026

China's New Parcel Standard Limits Packaging and Tape

China's mandatory standard restricting excessive express-delivery packaging took effect July 1. It sets baseline limits for parcel size, packaging layers and sealing-tape use, targeting oversized boxes and unnecessary material around e-commerce shipments.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Lower material allowances increase the importance of consistent film and tape width, accurate perforation and clean edges because converters have less excess material available to mask variation.

Source: Packaging Insights on China's express-packaging standard

3Packaging Material ReductionJune 23, 2026

Carrefour Plans to Remove 5,000 Tonnes of Plastic

Carrefour plans to eliminate 5,000 tonnes of plastic packaging through lighter multipacks, refill formats, paper substitutions, reuse and redesigned bakery packs. The retailer says virgin-plastic prices have risen 50% and expects more than EUR 5 million in savings.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Material reduction and paper substitution will create more short runs and new web structures, favoring lines with low setup waste, recipe control and dependable handling across film and paper grades.

Source: Packaging Insights on Carrefour's packaging-reduction plan

4Film DowngaugingJune 18, 2026

Graphene Masterbatch Targets 20% Film Downgauging

NanoXplore and Techmer PM are commercializing a graphene-enhanced masterbatch for packaging films. The partners report up to 70% higher mechanical strength and up to 20% thickness reduction, with validation available on a five-layer blown-film line.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Thinner, stronger films reduce material cost but become more tension-sensitive, so slitters and rewinders need fine low-tension control, static management and reliable roll-start and roll-finish handling.

Source: Packaging Insights on NanoXplore and Techmer PM film technology

5Healthcare Film CoatingJune 5, 2026

Amcor Adds Precision Coating Capacity in Asia

Amcor expanded healthcare-packaging capacity in India and opened a US$35 million coating facility in Malaysia. The Malaysian site uses air-knife coating for uniform web coverage and supports sterile packaging, local trials and pilot-to-production scale-up.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Medical webs require tight coating uniformity and defect control through every downstream step, making inspection, contamination control and traceable slitting and rewinding essential acceptance criteria.

Source: Packaging Insights on Amcor's India and Malaysia expansion
