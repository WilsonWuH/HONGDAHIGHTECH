1Recycled Cartonboard SupplyAugust 17, 2026

MM Board & Paper Signs Agreement to Acquire R.D.M. Arnsberg Recycled Cartonboard Mill

MM Board & Paper signed an agreement with the R.D.M. Group to acquire the operating business of the R.D.M. Arnsberg recycled fibre-based cartonboard mill in Germany through an asset deal. The mill has an annual production capacity of about 230,000 tonnes of recycled fibre-based cartonboard and specializes in liner products. MM expects the integration into its existing German cartonboard and packaging platform to create synergies, increase production flexibility, improve security of supply and broaden its product and service offering. All employees transfer to MM, the purchase price is undisclosed, and closing is expected in the fourth quarter of 2026 subject to antitrust approval.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Recycled cartonboard supply is consolidating into larger producer networks, so boxboard and carton converters should requalify roll grades, delivery terms and liner specifications as ownership changes.

Source: EQS News on MM Board & Paper acquisition

2Tissue CapacityAugust 14, 2026

TNPL Begins Commercial Tissue Paper Production in Tiruchirapalli

Tamil Nadu Newsprint and Papers Limited (TNPL) inaugurated commercial production at its tissue paper unit in Mondipatti, Tiruchi district, an investment of about INR 350 crore using the latest international technology. The unit will produce facial, napkin, towel and toilet tissue, marketed under the Lumia brand. TNPL, one of India's major newsprint and paper producers, is entering the tissue business as a growth move while retaining its established printing and writing paper lines.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: New Indian tissue parent-roll and converted-product supply adds regional sourcing options, so converters should track grade consistency, roll widths and delivery lead times before shifting suppliers.

Source: The Hindu on TNPL tissue production

3Hygiene Paper ModernizationAugust 16, 2026

SHP Harmanec Plans Comprehensive Modernization of Slovak Hygiene Paper Plant

SHP Harmanec, a Slovak producer of hygiene paper products, is preparing a comprehensive modernization of its plant in Harmanec, whose papermaking history dates to 1829. Environmental impact assessment documents show the first complete replacement of the production setup in more than five decades: aging technology is causing unplanned stoppages and higher maintenance, while the company wants greater production flexibility, a wider range of hygiene products at lower cost and higher quality, plus reduced energy, water and emissions. The plan includes full technology replacement, a new warehouse and wastewater treatment, and a shift from steam boilers toward biomass. SHP Group management previously confirmed an estimated investment of roughly EUR 200 million, with technology replacement continuing to 2033.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: A multi-year modernization of a hygiene paper mill changes grade mix and roll availability, so converters should confirm continuity of supply and requalify parent rolls as new lines start up.

Source: TASR via teraz.sk on SHP Harmanec modernization

4Supply ContinuityAugust 14, 2026

Andhra Paper Restores Kadiam Unit to Full Capacity with Third Machine Restart

Andhra Paper fully restored manufacturing operations at its Kadiam unit in East Godavari, Andhra Pradesh, after the third paper machine resumed operations on August 14, 2026. The machine has a production capacity of 15 tonnes per day, and its restart returns the unit to 100 percent capacity. The mill produces printing and writing paper grades that feed Indian institutional and converting markets.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Full restoration of a regional paper mill removes a supply constraint, so converters should re-verify allocation, pricing and lead times before locking in contracts for the coming quarters.

Source: Papermart on Andhra Paper capacity restoration

5Sack Kraft ConvertingAugust 14, 2026

Mondi and Fixit Gruppe Standardize European Paper Valve Bag Portfolio

Mondi collaborated with building materials supplier Fixit Gruppe to create a unified European paper bag portfolio covering more than 100 products, replacing multiple bag grades and formats. After an audit at Mondi's Bag Application Centre in Frantschach, Austria, Mondi standardized paper grammage, barrier protection and plastic inner-layer thickness for the new paper valve bags, which provide moisture protection for cement and powdered products while filling reliably on Fixit's machinery. The streamlined portfolio optimizes material use, supports recycling and cuts greenhouse gas emissions by about 135 tonnes of CO2 per year.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Standardized sack kraft and valve bag formats reduce grade proliferation, so converters should confirm their slitting, sealing and bag-making lines match the new grammages and barrier specifications.

Source: Druckspiegel on Mondi-Fixit paper bag portfolio

1Roll Supply RecoveryAugust 12, 2026

MENANG Restarts Malaysian Automotive Nonwoven Output with New ANDRITZ Airlay Line

MENANG, a Malaysian manufacturer of nonwoven automotive components, resumed production after a major fire severely damaged its airlay line in mid-March 2026. The company ordered a new ANDRITZ airlay line at the end of March, and installation and commissioning were completed by mid-July through a compressed project schedule. By running site reconstruction and equipment installation in parallel, MENANG restarted production within about four months, restoring continuity for its customers and adding modern web-forming technology for automotive applications.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Restored automotive nonwoven roll supply removes a regional sourcing risk, and the fast rebuild shows how line speed, installation time and supplier support matter when production is interrupted.

Source: ANDRITZ on MENANG airlay line restart

2Demand & Supply DataAugust 17, 2026

China H1 Nonwoven Output Rises 10.1% as Exports Grow 14.8%

According to the China Nonwovens and Industrial Textiles Association and National Bureau of Statistics data, output of nonwovens by Chinese enterprises above designated size rose 10.1 percent in the first half of 2026, with nonwoven exports up 14.8 percent to 938,000 tonnes. Nonwoven roll exports reached US$2.38 billion, up 13.1 percent, while absorbent product exports (diapers and sanitary pads) rose 13.5 percent and wipes exports rose 16.5 percent. The nonwovens sector's revenue grew 7.8 percent and profits grew 23.2 percent, with sample capacity utilization at 79.4 percent, up 3.2 percentage points.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Rising Chinese nonwoven output and exports mean more roll supply and sharper competition for wipes and hygiene converters, so buyers should re-benchmark pricing, lead times and grade consistency from new supply sources.

Source: China Textile News on H1 industrial textiles data

3Sustainable Roll CapacityAugust 7, 2026

Suominen Starts Qualification of Alicante Sustainable Nonwovens Line as Q2 Sales Rise

Suominen's second-quarter 2026 net sales rose 5.8 percent to EUR 105.6 million and comparable EBITDA reached EUR 4.3 million, up from EUR 3.2 million a year earlier, supported by higher production volumes and early operational efficiency gains. At the end of the quarter, after resolving technical issues, the company started qualification of its new production line in Alicante, Spain, dedicated to sustainable nonwovens. Suominen's Full Potential Program, launched in January 2026 and targeting a 10 percent EBITDA margin, moved from planning to execution during the period.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: New sustainable nonwoven roll capacity in Europe is entering qualification, so wipes and hygiene converters should watch substrate availability and re-verify tensile, softness and winding behavior before switching roll goods.

Source: Suominen H1 2026 interim report

4Medical Nonwoven ConsolidationAugust 3, 2026

Freudenberg Acquires Foshan United Medical to Expand Advanced Wound Care

Freudenberg Performance Materials acquired Foshan United Medical (UMT) in Foshan, Guangdong, China, from its founder and a private founding partner. UMT is a full-service supplier of advanced wound care products whose capabilities span fibre manufacturing and functionalization, nonwovens production, final packaging, sterilization and marketing authorization. Its regulatory know-how and first approvals in China, Europe and the United States will expand Freudenberg's healthcare business, with products including polyurethane foam dressings, silicone-coated and antimicrobial silver dressings, and alginate, chitosan and CMC absorbent dressings.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Consolidation in medical nonwovens changes supplier capabilities and qualification requirements, so converters serving wound care should re-verify material certifications, sterilization compatibility and roll-to-roll converting parameters.

Source: Innovation in Textiles on Freudenberg acquisition

5Hygiene Demand RiskAugust 4, 2026

Kimberly-Clark Q2: China Diaper Disruption Weighs on 2026 Outlook

Kimberly-Clark reported its tenth consecutive quarter of positive volume-plus-mix performance in Q2 2026 but lowered its full-year outlook, citing disruptions in its China diaper business, retailer inventory reductions in North America, a Los Angeles distribution center fire and higher Middle East-related costs. Management said the China disruption, which began in late June following claims the company says lack scientific support, is being handled through certified third-party laboratory testing, cooperation with Chinese authorities and consumer communications. The company expects the China issue to reduce second-half operating profit by about US$70 million, while North America shipments declined 1.4 percent as consumption rose 0.3 percent.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Quality and safety claims can disrupt hygiene demand and supply chains quickly, so converters should keep compliance documentation, batch traceability and third-party test evidence ready while monitoring diaper material order patterns.

Source: Nasdaq on Kimberly-Clark Q2 earnings call

1Regulation & ComplianceAugust 12, 2026

EU PPWR Now in Force: PFAS Restrictions and Conformity Duties Apply from August 12

The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR, Regulation (EU) 2025/40) entered into force on August 12, 2026, replacing the previous packaging directive with directly applicable obligations. PFAS in food-contact packaging is restricted immediately at specified concentration thresholds, and packaging manufacturers must carry out conformity assessment, prepare technical documentation and issue an EU declaration of conformity before placing packaging on the market, retaining records for five years for single-use and ten years for reusable packaging. Recyclability becomes a baseline requirement with performance grades from 2030, minimum recycled content targets apply from 2030, and non-compliant goods risk rejection at EU borders.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Film and flexible packaging converters selling into the EU must now document conformity, eliminate PFAS-based grease-resistant coatings and plan recyclable or mono-material structures, which changes film formulations, lamination and slitting requirements.

Source: BCLP on new EU packaging rules

2Flexible Packaging CapacityAugust 7, 2026

Amcor Adds 7,000 m2 Flexible Packaging Facility and Automated Warehouse in Dongguan

Amcor is expanding its flexible packaging solutions facility in Dongguan, China, with a 7,000-square-metre manufacturing facility and automated warehouse, enlarging the campus to more than 38,000 square metres. The project strengthens supply chain resilience in South China and is expected to be completed by July 2027. Amcor says the expansion adds technologies and capabilities to create a more efficient and intelligent manufacturing ecosystem for customers serving consumer markets in the region.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: New flexible packaging and film converting capacity in South China increases regional supply competition, so converters should compare lead times, automation levels and service capabilities when planning capacity or sourcing decisions.

Source: Interplas Insights on Amcor Dongguan expansion

3PCR Film Resin SupplyAugust 17, 2026

Dow Thailand Launches Commercial REVOLOOP PCR Resins for Stretch and Shrink Film

Dow Thailand has started commercial sales of PCR resins produced in Thailand from post-consumer plastic waste, marketed under the REVOLOOP brand. Two grades are available for film packaging: a 100 percent PCR grade for stretch film and an 80 percent PCR grade for shrink film and general blown film. The 80 percent grade uses Dow's resin technology to improve mechanical properties, appearance and defect reduction while maintaining strength, toughness, flexibility and clarity. Dow says it worked with Thai film producers and brands on commercial applications, extending its PCR portfolio in Asia-Pacific.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Regionally produced PCR film resins expand recycled-content options, so stretch and shrink film converters should requalify elongation, tension profiles and defect levels before switching lines to PCR grades.

Source: The Bangkok Insight on Dow REVOLOOP PCR resins

4Recycled Content DemandAugust 14, 2026

PepsiCo Raises Recycled Content in Plastic Packaging as Flexibles Shift to Mono-Material

PepsiCo reported using 2 million metric tons of plastic in packaging during 2025, slightly less than in 2024, with a 6 percent reduction in virgin plastic tonnage following a 5 percent reduction in 2024. Recycled content in its plastic packaging rose to 18 percent from 15 percent, against a target of 40 percent or greater by 2035. PET bottles account for 71 percent of PepsiCo's plastic packaging footprint and multimaterial flexibles for 12 percent, while flexible films represent 5 percent of overall packaging materials. The company cited lightweighting of beverage bottles, a switch of select chip multipacks from plastic overwraps to paper boxes in Australia, and molded fiber bottle trials as structural moves.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Large brand owners are shifting flexible packaging toward mono-material and verified recycled-content structures, so film converters should qualify PCR and recyclable film grades to retain food and snack packaging orders.

Source: Packaging Dive on PepsiCo packaging report

5BOPET/Release Film CapacityAugust 17, 2026

Jiemei Adds RMB 428 Million to North China BOPET and Release Film Base

Zhejiang Jiemei Electronic Technology approved an additional RMB 428 million investment in its North China R&D and production base, raising total project investment to no more than RMB 1.878 billion. Release film capacity will rise from 480 million square metres per year to 768 million square metres per year, alongside 20,000 tonnes per year of BOPET base film, 880,000 rolls of thin carrier tape and 130,000 rolls of cover tape. The company cites rising MLCC demand from AI servers, new energy vehicles and robotics, with production scheduled to come on stream in phases by the end of 2027.

Why it matters for converters and machine buyers: Expanding BOPET and release film capacity creates more roll-based converting demand and new supply sources, so converters should track grade availability and prepare slitting, rewinding and roll handling for larger base-film volumes.

Source: Guandian on Jiemei BOPET and release film investment
