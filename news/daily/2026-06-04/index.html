1Nonwoven WipesJune 4, 2026

ANDRITZ Wins Another Dalian Ruiguang Flushable Wipes Line Order

What happened

ANDRITZ said on May 20, 2026 that Dalian Ruiguang in China ordered another Wetlace line for premium flushable wipes roll goods. The line is scheduled to start up at the end of 2026.

Capacity impact

ANDRITZ said the new line will lift Dalian Ruiguang's total annual production capacity to 40,000 tons. The company said the wipes material is pulp-based, biodegradable, and designed to meet IWSFG and JIS P 4501 flushability requirements.

Why it matters

This is a direct signal that investment in sustainable hygiene substrates remains active in China. For machinery suppliers and converters, the order shows continued demand for flushable wipe materials that can meet tighter performance and compliance standards.

2Wet Wipes PackagingJune 4, 2026

Mondi Launches 35 Percent PCR Flow Wrap for Wet Wipes

What happened

Mondi said on June 2, 2026 that it launched re/loop FlowWrap for wet wipes packaging. The company positioned the product as a more sustainable polymer-based replacement for non-recyclable laminates.

Material details

Mondi said the new form-fill-and-seal structure is made from mono-polypropylene and contains 35 percent post-consumer recycled content. The company said internal testing, customer trials, and external ILAPACK trials showed comparable sealing, barrier performance, and high-speed runnability on existing lines.

Why it matters

Wet wipes remain a demanding packaging segment because of seal integrity, barrier needs, and line speed. A recyclable mono-material structure with recycled content is directly relevant for converters facing PPWR-related design pressure in Europe and similar buyer demands elsewhere.

3PE FilmJune 4, 2026

Smart Planet Pushes HyperBarrier Mono-Material PE Film

What happened

Packaging Europe reported on May 11, 2026 that Smart Planet Technologies unveiled HyperBarrier PE film. Smart Planet describes the product as a recyclable mono-material polyethylene film for high-barrier flexible packaging.

Performance claims

Smart Planet says the film can deliver up to 300 times better oxygen barrier and 3 to 4 times better moisture barrier than conventional LDPE while staying compatible with PE recycling streams. The company says the film is designed to run on standard blown film, cast film, and extrusion coating lines without added metallization or laminations.

Why it matters

Barrier PE that stays in a PE recycling stream is one of the main technical targets in flexible packaging. If these claims hold in commercial trials, the product could reduce the need for more complex multilayer structures in food and other sensitive applications.

4Medical PackagingJune 4, 2026

DNP Launches Mono-Material Medical Sterilization Pouch

What happened

Dai Nippon Printing said on April 16, 2026 that it started supplying samples of a new mono-material medical sterilization pouch. The company said the pouch is designed to improve sortability and recyclability in line with CEFLEX guidance.

Product details

DNP said the pouch is made from more than 90 wt% polyethylene-based materials and is compatible with EOG, gamma, and hydrogen peroxide gas sterilization. The company said its converting technology enabled peel-open performance and seal strength comparable to conventional medical sterilization packaging.

Why it matters

Medical packaging has moved more slowly on recyclability than consumer packaging. This launch shows that mono-material development is now reaching regulated packaging segments where sealing performance and clean opening are critical.

5Nonwoven InvestmentJune 4, 2026

Kruger Commits $333 Million to Enter Wipe Nonwovens

What happened

Kruger said on May 11, 2026 that it will invest $333 million to enter the wipe nonwovens sector. The company said the new business will focus on sustainable materials for wipe applications.

Project scope

The project centers on a new production line in Trois-Rivieres, Quebec, next to Kruger's Wayagamack paper mill. Kruger said the line will be the first of its kind in Canada and is scheduled for commissioning in 2028.

Why it matters

The move shows pulp and paper groups still see growth in wipe substrates and adjacent hygiene materials. It also links nonwoven expansion to fiber-based and plastic-free positioning, which matters for buyers tracking sustainability claims in wipes.

6Tissue PaperJune 4, 2026

Metsa Group Inaugurates Expanded Mariestad Tissue Mill

What happened

Metsa Group said on May 28, 2026 that it inaugurated its expanded and modernized tissue paper mill in Mariestad, Sweden. The company held the opening ceremony on May 26.

Investment scale

Metsa said the Mariestad project represents a 370 million euro investment and doubles annual production capacity to about 145,000 tonnes. The upgraded site includes a new tissue machine and three new converting lines for rolled and folded tissue products.

Why it matters

This is one of the largest recent European tissue investments and a direct sign that hygiene paper producers are still funding new converting capacity close to regional demand. It also reflects the push to pair higher throughput with better water and energy performance.

7Tissue ConvertingJune 4, 2026

Domtar Starts Installing High-Speed Tissue Converting Line in Tennessee

What happened

Domtar said on February 16, 2026 that its Calhoun, Tennessee site began installing a new high-speed tissue converting line. The company said the project is intended to raise output, improve efficiency, and support future scale.

Site scope

Domtar also said it is expanding warehouse space for parent tissue rolls at Calhoun. The project is meant to align tissue production capacity more closely with converting capability at the site.

Why it matters

For tissue converters, this is a clear example of North American producers investing in downstream finishing rather than only base-sheet capacity. It also points to continued pressure to improve service levels and operating flexibility in the US tissue market.

8Paper & TissueJune 4, 2026

Sofidel Finalizes $775 Million Tissue Expansion in Oklahoma

What happened

Sofidel said on March 11, 2026 that it finalized the details of a $775 million expansion at its Inola, Oklahoma integrated facility. The project supports the group's continued buildup in the United States.

Project scope

Sofidel said the plan includes a new building for a 75,000-tonne-per-year Valmet TAD tissue machine, converting lines with matching finished-goods capacity, expanded pulp and parent reel storage, and a fully automated finished-goods warehouse with 100,000 pallet positions.

Why it matters

This is a large-scale North American tissue investment that combines base paper, converting, and logistics in one site plan. For equipment and material suppliers, it shows that premium tissue capacity and automation spending remain active in the US South.

9Film ConvertingJune 4, 2026

Nobelus Expands Film Converting with Catbridge Duplex Slitter Rewinder

What happened

Catbridge said on February 19, 2026 that Nobelus installed a Model 900 Duplex Slitter Rewinder at its Elgin, Illinois facility. The supplier said the installation expands Nobelus' converting capability for film and laminate products.

Equipment details

Catbridge said the machine handles webs up to 84 inches wide and runs up to 2,500 feet per minute. The company said the system is designed to process larger and heavier rolls while improving tension control, roll quality, and changeover efficiency.

Why it matters

For slitting and rewinding buyers, this is a clean signal that North American converters are still investing in throughput and automation on the finishing side. That matters for suppliers serving flexible packaging, label, and laminated film markets.

10Mono-Material FilmJune 4, 2026

UFlex Introduces Heat-Sealable Mono-Material PET Film

What happened

UFlex introduced its F-HSS film at PlastIndia 2026, according to a February 9, 2026 company press note. The company said the transparent biaxially oriented polyester film is designed to support mono-material PET packaging structures.

Film specification

UFlex said F-HSS uses a co-extruded PET structure with a copolymer-based sealant layer and delivers seal strength above 1.5 kg per inch. The company said the film is aimed at lidding for APET, RPET, and CPET trays, robust food pouches, and mono-material PET packs for dry snacks.

Why it matters

Mono-material packaging development is not limited to PE and PP. UFlex is pushing PET-based alternatives that may fit applications where clarity, seal strength, and machinability are critical for converters and brand owners.
